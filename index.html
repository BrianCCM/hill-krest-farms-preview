Three directions for the website

Three different personalities for the same venue. Open each one, scroll the whole page, and tell us which feels most like the farm. We will build the rest of the site in whichever you pick.

Nothing here is final. Everything marked in this colour is a placeholder waiting on information from you, and no number, review or policy on these pages is real.

One. Heirloom Your pick

Open Heirloom

The quiet one. Ivory paper, deep olive green and a lot of open space, with the headline set large and light in the middle of the page. It reads like the front of a wedding magazine, and it is the direction that most says this venue is worth paying for.

Uses the arbor and botanical logo. Best if you want couples planning a dressy, considered wedding. Least obviously country of the three.

This is the one you chose, so it is the only one carrying the new photographs and the real copy. Two and three are earlier drafts kept for reference and still show the original phone photos and placeholder text.

Two. Golden Hour

Open Golden Hour

The emotional one. Dark and warm, built around the photograph of the barn lit up at dusk, which fills the whole screen before you read a word. Amber accents, and every photo graded to the same evening warmth.

Uses the plain bronze wordmark, so nothing competes with the pictures. This is the direction that performs best when someone finds you on a phone through Instagram. It leans hardest on photography, so it has the most to gain from better photos.

Three. Homestead

Open Homestead

The friendly one. Cream and barn red, heavier type, visible borders and boxes, and your capacity numbers right under the headline where nobody has to hunt for them. Warmest and most direct of the three.

Uses the barn illustration logo. Best if you want reunions, company dinners and community events as much as weddings, because it reads as welcoming rather than exclusive.

What is still missing

Every direction is running on the same placeholder content, because we would rather leave an honest blank than invent something you have to walk back later. To finish the one you choose we need:

Capacity, split out

We have the 100 to 150 total. Still need how many of those seat indoors versus on the lawn.

Pricing

Even a starting-at figure. Venue shoppers filter on price first, and a page with no number gets skipped rather than emailed.

Counts

How many tables and chairs, what size the tents are, how many vehicles park, and how many restrooms.

Policies

Catering, music curfew, open flame, the rain plan and clean-up. Alcohol has been left off the site.

Phone and email

The address and both social accounts are in. Still need the number and the inbox that inquiries should reach.

Real reviews

Three to five, with a first name and event type. Screenshots are fine. Nothing invented goes in that space.

Photographer permission and credit

The new professional photographs have transformed the site, and they are now doing most of the work on the Heirloom pages. Before this goes live we need written permission from whoever shot them, and the name to credit. Wedding photographers normally grant a venue this happily, but it does need asking, and the credit line is usually part of the deal.

Two gaps are still worth filling when you get a chance: the hall empty and clean in daylight, and the getting-ready space if you have one. Couples look for both.
