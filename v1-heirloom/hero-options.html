Every hero we have tried

Four in one place so they can be compared rather than remembered. The first three are the same layout with a different photograph. The fourth changes the layout itself, which is worth a look if none of the photographs have felt right.

Option A

The ceremony lawn Currently live

The arbor dressed in red and cream, chairs set in two blocks, the aisle mown down the middle, the milk can and the pampas. Nobody in it, so nothing had to be cropped away, and it is the only photograph that shows a guest exactly what they will walk into. The haze in the sky that day means the gradient reads as morning mist rather than a wash.

Option B

The farm truck

Sells the setting. The truck and geraniums, the farm's own sign, the grain bin, the barn, the tent and guests arriving, all in one frame. A stranger understands what the place is before reading a word.

Option C

The couple and the ceiling

The barn hall cropped to the chandelier, the trusses and the couple, with every seated guest cut away. The quietest and most formal of the three, and the only one that shows the inside of the building.

Option D, a different layout

Words beside the picture, not above it

Every option above puts the headline above a wide strip of photograph, which is why each new picture has needed a different crop to fit. Setting the words beside the picture instead gives the photograph a tall column, close to the shape a camera actually produces. Almost nothing gets cropped, no gradient is needed, and the next photograph will drop in without any of this.

It also uses the file better. Across the full width this photograph is stretched to its exact pixel width and will look soft on a sharp screen; in a column it has room to spare.

One thing worth knowing

The ceremony photograph is a phone picture, 1440 pixels wide. Stretched across a desktop screen that is exactly its full size with nothing in reserve, so it will look a little soft on a modern laptop. The professional photographs are four times larger. If the ceremony lawn is the shot you want in the hero, it is worth asking the photographer whether they have one of the empty setup.
