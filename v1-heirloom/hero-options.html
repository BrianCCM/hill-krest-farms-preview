Three ways to handle the hero text

The problem is the lit doorway directly behind the headline. It is the brightest thing in the photograph, which is why a plain dark wash has to flatten the whole image to fix it. Each option below solves it a different way. Scroll through and tell us the letter.

Option A

Shaped scrim

The darkening is an oval centred on the text rather than a flat sheet over everything, so the trees, the lights and the foreground tables keep their colour while the hotspot behind the headline gets pushed down. Closest to what you have now, and the photograph stays the loudest thing on the page.

Option B

Ivory card

The words sit on a panel of the site's own ivory, floating over the photograph. Legibility stops depending on the picture entirely, so this keeps working if you swap the hero for a brighter summer shot later. The most formal of the three, and the most wedding-stationery. The trade is that the panel covers part of the barn.

Option C

Fade to ivory

The photograph rises out of the page rather than sitting in a box. The top fades into the same ivory as the rest of the site, the headline sits in that faded band in dark ink, and the picture is fully uncovered from the barn roof down. No scrim over the barn at all, so the dusk colour and the string lights are the strongest they can be.
